# WUZHEN — Waterways & Bridges

A ninth-grade English PBL (Project-Based Learning) presentation on why water is the main line of Wuzhen ancient town.

- **Group**: 907班 · 90706
- **Members**: 6
- **Project length**: 4 weeks
- **Delivery**: classroom presentation (English) + live Q&A
- **Audience**: teacher and classmates
- **Core question**: *Why is water the main line of the ancient town?*

---

## 01 — FACTS

Three facts that anchor everything the project says. All figures come from the project's own research notes.

### Fact 01 — Cross-shaped Water System
Wuzhen's inland water system forms a cross shape, dividing the old town into different zones. The waterways are a key part of the town's spatial layout.

> 乌镇的内河水系呈十字形，把古镇划分为不同区域，水道是古镇空间布局的重要组成部分。

Water divides the town into zones, and the town is also connected by water.

### Fact 02 — 72 ancient stone bridges, ~10,000 m of waterways
Xizha (西栅) has nearly **10,000 metres of waterways** and **72 ancient stone bridges**.

- **10,000 METERS** — of waterways in Xizha
- **72 BRIDGES** — ancient stone arches

> 西栅有近万米河道和72座古石桥，桥梁与水道共同构成典型的江南水乡景观。

Bridges connect the banks, making water accessible to everyone.

### Fact 03 — Water & Everyday Life
In the past, local people used boats for travel, transportation and trade.

> 过去当地居民常利用船只出行、运输和进行交易，水道直接参与人们的日常生活。

Water was not just scenery — it was the road of daily life.

## 04 — WHY WATER?

The argument the whole project rests on, drawn as a chain:

**WATER → CONNECTS → HOUSES · BRIDGES · STREETS · PEOPLE · BOATS → TRADITIONAL LIFE**

Water comes first; everything else has to follow it. Houses face the canal, bridges cross it, streets run along it, boats move on it. That chain is what makes Wuzhen a water town rather than a collection of old buildings.

## 07 — SIX CHAPTERS OF THE STORY

### 01 — WATER · 水
*WATER SHAPES WUZHEN*
Canals run through every corner of the town. They were the roads, and still are.

In Wuzhen the water came first. The canals were dug long before the streets were paved, and the houses were built facing them. To move from one side of town to the other, a family might walk along a stone embankment, or take a boat.

### 02 — BRIDGES · 桥
*BRIDGES LINK TWO BANKS*
Stone arches. Low steps. A crossing, and a view.

Wuzhen is criss-crossed by stone bridges. Many are old enough to remember the shape of a different town. A boat must duck beneath them, and a person climbing the steps looks down onto still water. The bridge is the one place where the town has to decide how to meet itself.

### 03 — BOATS · 船
*THE BOAT MOVES FORWARD*
A pole pushes against mud. The water answers.

The black-canopied boat — a wupeng — is the slowest and most patient way to travel here. It slips under the low arches, turns a corner, and keeps going. Watching a boat read the town like a sentence: the water is the line it is written on.

### 04 — LIFE · 生活
*LIFE HAPPENS BY WATER*
Laundry at the embankment. Shops, doors, footsteps.

People did not build a water town and then move in. They were already there, and the water became the place of daily work — where clothes were washed, where boats were tied up, where neighbours met. The street is the story of that ordinary life, repeated for a thousand mornings.

### 05 — NIGHT · 夜
*THE LIGHTS COME ON*
Lanterns over the water. The town darkens, then glows.

When evening falls, Wuzhen changes its colour. The stone cools to blue and the lanterns take over, pouring warm light onto the water and then off it again into reflections. Standing on a bridge at night, it is hard to tell where the town ends and its image begins.

### 06 — MEMORY · 记忆
*A PLACE SHAPED BY WATER* — bridges, and time.

The question this project began with — why is water the main line of the ancient town? — is answered by simply watching it work. Water carried the people, the boats, the light and the evening. A town built around water keeps its shape around water, for as long as the canals stay open.
